Naira Set for Strongest Annual Gain Since 2018 Despite Election Risk

business

By Staff Reporter · September 3, 2026

Nigeria's naira is on course for its strongest annual gain against the dollar since at least 2018, as rising oil prices and remittance inflows help insulate the currency from political risks tied to the 2027 election.

Nigeria's naira is heading for its strongest annual gain against the US dollar since at least 2018, as rising dollar inflows from higher oil prices and remittances help insulate the currency from the political risks typically associated with an election season, according to a Bloomberg report.

The naira is expected to end the year at around 1,290 per dollar, extending its roughly 8 percent gain so far this year to almost 12 percent, according to the average forecast of four analysts surveyed by Bloomberg. Formal diaspora remittances reached $3.8 billion in the first seven months of 2026, up 50.2 percent from a year earlier, while Nigeria's foreign reserves rose to about $53.9 billion by early September, their highest level in 18 years, up from $45.6 billion at the start of the year.

Analysts say the naira's 2026 recovery differs from previous currency gains driven mainly by central bank intervention, since Nigeria has also built a larger supply of foreign exchange through rising remittances, oil export earnings and capital inflows. The next major test for the currency will be the 2027 general election, which can typically bring higher government spending and increased dollar demand.
