Nepal-Tibet Flood Disaster: Death Toll Passes 550, Nearly 2,000 Still Missing

news

By Staff Reporter · August 28, 2026 · 1 min read

A glacier collapse that triggered catastrophic flash floods on the Nepal-Tibet border has killed more than 550 people, with nearly 2,000 still missing and rescue efforts hampered by the threat of a barrier lake bursting.

A catastrophic flash flood triggered by a glacier collapse on the Nepal-Tibet border has killed more than 550 people in Nepal, with the toll still rising and nearly 2,000 people unaccounted for, authorities said Friday, August 28, 2026.

The disaster struck on Wednesday, August 26, when a large section of glacier broke away in the Himalayas and sent a surge of ice, water, rock and mud roughly 30 feet high down river valleys along the Nepal-China border, burying villages and destroying roads, bridges and power infrastructure for miles downstream. Nepal's prime minister's office said 469 people had been confirmed dead as of Friday morning, a figure that continued climbing through the day, with police later citing 553 to 579 deaths and more than 1,900 people still missing. Chinese authorities reported five deaths on the Tibetan side of the border.

Officials say more than 640 of the missing in Nepal are foreign nationals, including dozens of Americans and at least 38 Australians, many reported to be Hindu pilgrims caught in the disaster zone. About 1,550 people have been rescued so far. Rescue operations have been repeatedly interrupted by fears that two glacial lakes formed by debris upstream could burst and unleash further flooding; Nepal's Disaster Management Authority said the risk would persist until both lakes are safely drained. U.S. President Donald Trump said Washington was willing to send "any help" to Nepal, while aid groups including the International Federation of the Red Cross described urgent shortages of shelter, food and medical supplies in the flood zone.
