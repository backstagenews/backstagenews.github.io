14-Year-Old Nigerian Wins Inaugural World Amateur Rapid Chess Championship

sports

By Staff Reporter · August 11, 2026 · 2 min read

Goodness Ekunke, 14, beat a field of far more experienced players to win the first-ever FIDE World Amateur Rapid Chess Championship in Abuja.

Nigeria has a new global chess champion — and he's only 14 years old. Goodness Ekunke, known in local chess circles as "Boss Baby," won the inaugural FIDE World Amateur Rapid Chess Championship in Abuja, finishing the 11-round tournament with 9 points out of a possible 11.

The Bayelsa-born teenager beat back a strong international field that included far more experienced players, several of them senior FIDE-titled masters. Two fellow Nigerians, FM Eugene Unwana and FM Bomo Kigigha, took second and third place, each finishing on 8½ points — meaning Nigerian players swept the podium.

The tournament had late drama. Seventeen-year-old Joseph Sambo briefly seized control of the standings in the eighth round after upsetting Ekunke, then knocked out Kigigha and Unwana in later rounds before his own challenge faded, leaving him fourth. Unwana surged back with wins over Kigigha and Sambo to close the gap, setting up a winner-takes-all final round against Ekunke. The two Nigerians fought to a draw — enough for Ekunke to hold on and claim the historic title.

Organised jointly by FIDE and the Nigeria Chess Federation, the championship was played with a 15-minute time control and a 10-second increment per move. Ekunke, who became Nigeria's national Under-14 champion at just eight years old, is now being talked about as a future Grandmaster prospect — a milestone only a handful of Sub-Saharan African players have ever reached.

The win adds to a period of steady growth for chess in Nigeria, with federal and state-level programmes increasingly investing in school chess clubs, youth coaching, and international tournament exposure for promising young players. Ekunke himself has been coached within that same pipeline, having progressed from national age-group titles to senior international competition in the space of a few years.

Should he continue his trajectory toward the Grandmaster title, Ekunke would become just the sixth player from Sub-Saharan Africa to hold it — a reflection of how rare, and how significant, this week's result is for Nigerian chess.
