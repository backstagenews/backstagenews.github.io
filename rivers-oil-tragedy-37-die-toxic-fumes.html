Rivers Oil Tragedy: 37 Die From Toxic Fumes During Illegal Oil Loading

news

By Staff Reporter · September 3, 2026

At least 37 people died after inhaling toxic fumes during an illegal petroleum loading operation at Okari Jetty in Okrika, Rivers State, with many more feared missing.

At least 37 people died in the early hours of Thursday, September 3, 2026, after inhaling toxic fumes while illegally extracting petroleum products at the Okari Jetty in Okrika Local Government Area of Rivers State.

More than 100 youths from Okrika and neighbouring communities reportedly stormed the jetty in locally made boats around midnight to load petroleum product from an illegally tapped point, while a vessel was simultaneously loading the same product for export. A strong smell suddenly saturated the air during the operation, overwhelming and choking many of the victims, most of whom were young people. Some collapsed into the river, while survivors suffered severe respiratory distress and were rushed to hospital.

The spokesperson for the Rivers State Police Command, ASP Blessing Agabe, confirmed the incident and said an investigation had been opened. The Youth and Environmental Advocacy Centre (YEAC-Nigeria) confirmed at least 37 deaths through its local networks and called on the National Oil Spill Detection and Response Agency to conduct a Joint Investigation Visit to the site. Community members have called for the creation of alternative livelihoods for youths in the area to reduce the risks associated with illegal oil bunkering.
