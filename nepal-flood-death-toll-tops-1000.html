Nepal-Tibet Flood Death Toll Tops 1,000 as Search Continues for Trapped Workers

news

By Staff Reporter · September 1, 2026

The death toll from last week's catastrophic Nepal-Tibet flash floods has topped 1,000, with rescuers racing to reach hundreds of workers believed trapped in hydropower tunnels and nearly 4,400 people still missing.

The death toll from last week's catastrophic flash floods on the Nepal-Tibet border has topped 1,000, with rescuers racing to free hundreds of workers believed trapped in hydropower tunnels buried by mud and debris.

Nepal's disaster authority says more than 900 people have been confirmed dead in the country, with 4,247 people still missing, including 592 foreign nationals. China separately reported 16 deaths and 546 missing in its Tibet region. A private-sector association representing power companies said at least 900 to 933 workers remain unaccounted for at around a dozen hydropower projects damaged by the floods in Nepal's Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts, with several hundred believed trapped underground.

The disaster was triggered by a glacier collapse on August 26, sending a surge of ice, rock, mud and water down river valleys with what Chinese state broadcaster CCTV described as "severe destructive force." More than 10,000 people have been rescued so far, including over 320 foreign nationals. Rescue crews from Nepal, China and India are using excavators, controlled blasts and searchlights around the clock to clear debris from blocked tunnels, while mass burials continue for the hundreds of unidentified bodies recovered.
