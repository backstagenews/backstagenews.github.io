Nigerian Doctors Report Breakthrough in Colorectal Cancer Screening

news

By Staff Reporter · September 4, 2026

A research team at Obafemi Awolowo University Teaching Hospital has found that 74 percent of colorectal cancer cases are now detected at a curable stage, double the historical rate, through improved public awareness of warning symptoms.

A research team led by Dr Olusegun Alatise, a professor of surgery at Obafemi Awolowo University Teaching Hospital in Ile-Ife, has reported that improved public awareness campaigns have doubled the rate at which colorectal cancer is caught at a curable stage in Nigeria, according to a CNN report.

The team, working through the African Research Group for Oncology (ARGO), found that 74 percent of cases were detected at a potentially curable stage, up from roughly half historically. Their research also established that rectal bleeding alone did not reliably predict colorectal cancer, but did so when combined with weight loss and a change in bowel habits, a finding that helps doctors allocate scarce screening resources in a country where a colonoscopy can cost half the average monthly income.

Alatise's group is now working with Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and the University of Alberta to develop a cheaper urine-based screening test, and has launched one of only a handful of immunotherapy trials ever conducted in sub-Saharan Africa, using a drug called tislelizumab that appears especially effective in patients with a genetic signature three times more common in Nigeria than in the United States. Dr Peter Kingham, a collaborating surgeon, said changing diets, particularly the shift toward processed foods, are a major driver of the disease's rise across the region.
